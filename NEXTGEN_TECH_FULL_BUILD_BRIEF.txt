01. BUSINESS POSITIONING, AUDIENCES AND CONVERSION
=================================================
Proposed positioning:
"Practical technology consulting, custom applications and AI implementation for
businesses that need systems they can use and maintain."

The website must answer within its first screen:
- Who is this? Nextgen Tech Sdn Bhd.
- What does it do? Consulting, application development, AI agents/integration, AI training.
- How can the visitor proceed? Explore a service or describe the project.

Primary audiences and what each needs:
Business owner/SME operator: understand a practical outcome, scope and how to start.
Operations manager: understand workflow integration, staff adoption and support.
Technical decision-maker: see delivery practices, access controls and handover approach.
HR/L&D/training coordinator: see learning outcomes, audience level and training format.
Early-stage founder: understand product discovery, MVP development and iteration.
These audience groups are planning assumptions, not claims about current customers.

One main site-wide action: "Discuss your project".
Service-specific actions: "Discuss consulting", "Plan an application",
"Discuss an AI workflow" and "Enquire about training".
All route to the enquiry form with editable service context. Avoid five competing
buttons in one section or forcing visitors into a booking tool before they understand.

Proposed success measures:
- Qualified enquiries by service, with useful problem descriptions.
- Enquiry completion rate and form error/drop-off rate.
- Movement from service page to enquiry, and follow-up status internally.
- Case-study engagement where approved studies exist.
- Training enquiries from appropriate audiences.
Pageviews and time-on-site are secondary; do not optimise for a longer journey.
No targets, ROI claims or response-time promises should be published without evidence.

Public website is not a SaaS dashboard, client portal, LMS or online store at launch.
Those may be separate future projects. Avoid unnecessary accounts and checkout flows.

surfaces, dark text and a controlled violet accent. Continue the starter's recognisable
direction while making the company's specific services and real work carry the design.
Public pages primarily Persuade; long case studies/articles Read; admin Operate.

Proposed tokens, superseding conflicting draft values only when implemented here:

Text             #22212B  primary reading colour
Secondary text   #605E69  supporting text, readable at normal sizes

Danger           #B42318  validation/failure with text/icon
Focus ring       #5145CD  visible outline with offset on light surfaces
Inverse surface  #252334  at most selected bands/footer, with appropriate light text
Never use low-contrast light grey for essential service explanations or form help.

Typography:
Manrope remains the proposed primary family to preserve the existing starter identity.
Use 400/500/600/700, self-host licensed WOFF2, font-display: swap and metric-aware fallback.
DM Mono may be used sparingly for actual technical metadata; never for all body copy.
Desktop hero 56-76 px, line height 1.05-1.12, letter spacing around -0.035em.
Mobile hero 36-46 px, line height 1.1-1.2. Body 16-18 px, line height 1.55-1.7.
Section heading 28-44 px; labels 14-16 px; avoid tiny uppercase technical eyebrows.
Keep paragraphs around 55-75 characters per line. Long service titles wrap naturally.
Do not reproduce the starter's extremely tight -0.09em tracking across the full site.

Layout:
- Shared maximum width 1200 px; hero may use up to 1280 px if composition needs it.
- Gutters: 20 px small phones, 24 px tablets, 40-48 px on wider screens.
- Twelve-column desktop grid; content-defined layout collapse around 900-1024 px.
- Mobile sections 48-64 px vertical space, desktop 80-112 px where appropriate.
- More space above a section heading than between heading and its explanation.
- Use editorial rows for services, a real image/screen for work, and readable lists
  for deliverables. Reserve cards for content that benefits from a grouped boundary.
- Avoid horizontal scrolling except intentionally accessible content such as a code block.

Component inventory:
SiteHeader, DesktopServiceMenu, MobileNav, SiteFooter, Container, SectionHeading,
PrimaryButton, TextLink, ServiceFinder, ServiceRow, DeliverableList, ProcessSteps,
CaseStudyTeaser, CaseStudyTemplate, EvidenceCaption, TrainingTrack, ArticleLayout,
FAQDisclosure, ContactForm, ConditionalFields, FormErrorSummary, EnquirySuccess,
EmptyContentState, ImageWithCaption, StatusNotice and admin DataTable/DetailPanel.
Use variants for emphasis and state; do not create unrelated page-specific controls.
All components define default, hover where relevant, focus, active, disabled, pending,
error and success behaviour. Disabled controls have an accessible explanation if needed.

Motion, following Emil Kowalski's interaction guidance:
- Purpose is feedback or continuity, never a delay before reading content.
- Pointer press scale around 0.98 over 120 ms; focus remains steady and obvious.
- Service panel switch: optional 140-180 ms opacity, no large content slide.
- Menu/popover: 160-220 ms transform/opacity from trigger origin.
- Modal: small opacity/scale entry with focus handling; no scale(0).
- Use cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1) for responsive entry and explicit property lists.
- Frequent keyboard-triggered actions remain immediate; no artificial reveal delay.
- Hover styling gated to suitable pointers. Reduced motion removes transforms and
  decorative transitions; content must be visible even before animation initialisation.
- No scroll hijacking, custom cursor, mandatory loading intro or parallax-heavy hero.

Accessibility target: WCAG 2.2 AA; test rather than claiming certification.
Provide semantic landmarks, one meaningful h1, logical heading order, skip link,
keyboard menus, focus restoration, readable contrast and labelled form controls.
Prefer 44 px or larger touch targets; test 200% zoom, reflow and error announcements.
Accordion questions are real buttons with expanded state; essential service content
is not hidden behind mouse-only interactions. Do not place text inside images.

Visual assets:
Use approved company identity and real work images when available. A temporary text
wordmark is acceptable in a prototype and must not be presented as an approved logo.
Use original product screens or labelled illustrative demonstrations for technical work.
Avoid generic humanoid robots, glowing AI brains and stock photos implying fake staff.
Do not reveal client data in screenshots. Keep consent/source records for all assets.
No invented trust logos, five-star testimonials, customer counters or results badges.

05. FRONTEND ARCHITECTURE, CONTENT AND SEO
========================================
Recommended target during implementation:
React + TypeScript + React Router framework mode using its Vite build integration.
Pre-render public marketing routes, with ssr:false where static hosting is selected.
This creates usable HTML at build time; a separate API handles enquiries/admin data.
It is an explicit evolution of the existing vanilla Vite starter, not a capability
already installed. Check current framework/Node requirements and pin compatible versions.
Official pre-rendering reference is linked at the end of this document.

Proposed implementation structure:
  apps/web/app/root.tsx                  site shell and route-level defaults
  apps/web/app/routes/                   page modules
  apps/web/app/components/               shared UI and page sections
  apps/web/app/features/contact/         form logic, validation and API client
  apps/web/app/features/service-finder/  scoped interactive service selection
  apps/web/app/styles/                   tokens, global styles and component styles
  apps/web/content/                      typed service/company/article content
  apps/web/public/                       approved static assets only
  apps/api/src/modules/leads/            enquiry intake and follow-up operations
  apps/api/src/modules/admin/            protected staff operations
  apps/api/src/modules/notifications/    retryable email delivery
  packages/contracts/                   shared request/response schemas
  docs/                                 content inventory, deployment and QA records
Folders are a target plan; do not treat this outline as existing application code.

Implementation principles:
Build reusable page templates from typed content rather than duplicating markup.
Keep service data in structured objects: title, slug, audience, problem, outcomes,
deliverables, process, FAQs, related work and CTA context.
Use local state for menus/tabs, URL state for shareable filters and server state only
for live API data. A marketing website does not need a large global state store.
Use accessible headless primitives selectively; match the project's actual visual tokens.
CSS variables + CSS modules are sufficient; do not add competing UI themes.

Rendering and deployment:
Enumerate dynamic case-study/article slugs in the pre-render build. Rebuild on content
publication; keep the previous valid deployment if a build fails. Do not put private
draft content, enquiry records or secret CMS tokens into generated HTML/data files.
Admin stays authenticated client/server functionality and is never pre-rendered with data.
Serve generated routes directly, with hosting rules for route data and static assets.
Configure a real 404 fallback; avoid returning the homepage with status 200 for all paths.
Do not read window/localStorage during build-time rendering; browser behaviour begins
after hydration. Verify no hydration errors and that primary reading works without JS.

Content management stages:
Stage 1: validated TypeScript/JSON/Markdown content in source control, edited through
the development workflow. The website can launch without a custom CMS.
Stage 2: editor-friendly CMS with drafts, preview, versioning, media and publication.
Use a controlled build trigger for public content updates; do not publish an unreviewed
draft because a webhook arrived. Track content state separately from deployment state.
Choose one source of truth per content type; do not keep competing database/static copies.

SEO and discoverability:
Each route has a distinct accurate title/description, canonical URL, social preview,
logical headings and descriptive links. Final domain must be confirmed first.
Generate sitemap.xml from published, canonical routes and an intentional robots.txt.
Structured data describes only verified organisation/service/article facts. Do not
invent AggregateRating, awards, addresses, opening times or a LocalBusiness office.
Private/admin/thank-you/search-preview routes are excluded from indexing as appropriate.
When BM content exists, implement locale URLs, translated metadata, language links and
hreflang; do not auto-publish an unreviewed translation of technical/legal commitments.

Performance targets, not measured results:
LCP <= 2.5 s, INP <= 200 ms and CLS <= 0.1 at the 75th percentile where field data is
available. Use lab checks before launch and field monitoring once traffic exists.
Initial public-route JavaScript budget: aim below 180 KB gzip; explain exceptions.
Use responsive AVIF/WebP images with dimensions; prioritise only the actual hero asset,
lazy-load below-the-fold images, subset fonts and avoid a third-party widget waterfall.
Code-split admin and optional interactive demonstrations. A visible service explanation
must not depend on a slow analytics, chat, font or scheduling provider.

06. BACKEND FEATURES AND LEAD OPERATIONS
======================================
Launch backend scope: reliable enquiry intake, durable lead storage, abuse protection,
notification delivery and an authorised way for Ryan/staff to follow up.
Do not create a large CRM or public account system before validating need.

LEAD INTAKE
Validate all fields server-side; record service interest and optional structured answers.
Apply request size limits, basic honeypot/time checks and rate limiting. Add a challenge
only if abuse warrants it; keep an accessible alternative and avoid silent loss of
legitimate enquiries sharing an office IP.
Use an idempotency key scoped to submission token/session and payload hash. Repeated
requests return the same reference; a key reused for different content yields conflict.
Do not suppress every future enquiry from the same email; repeat business is valid.

Within one database transaction, create lead and notification outbox job.
Return success after durable storage, without waiting for an external email provider.
Worker sends staff notification and optional acknowledgement; retries with backoff,
records provider IDs/status and exposes permanently failed delivery to operations.
Escaping and safe email templates prevent header/HTML injection. Visitor address may
be used as validated Reply-To, not a forged From domain.
The staff inbox/lead database is the source of truth; an email outage must not lose leads.

LEAD WORKFLOW
Statuses: new -> triaged -> contacted -> qualified -> proposal -> won/lost/closed.
Allow disqualified/spam with reason; allow controlled reopening. Each change records
actor, timestamp and reason, with a version guard against concurrent updates.
Fields: owner, service interest, contact details, description, optional qualification
fields, internal notes, next action and follow-up date. No automated outreach beyond
the acknowledgement until a business process is explicitly configured.
Lead priority is a human workflow aid. Do not infer protected traits, scrape personal
profiles or generate an opaque "client worth" score.

ADMIN MVP
Roles: owner/admin and lead manager; content editor later. Roles are server-enforced.
Require login and MFA for privileged access. No shared hardcoded admin password.
Screens: lead list with filters/search, lead detail, notes/status/follow-up controls,
notification failures and audit history. Paginate; avoid loading the whole database.
Bulk export restricted and audited; spreadsheet exports must neutralise formula injection.
Show private records only after authorisation; hiding the /admin link is not protection.

CONTENT/CMS LATER
Company profile, service pages, case studies, training programmes, articles, FAQs,
navigation, media and SEO fields. Draft -> in_review -> approved -> published -> archived.
Record author/reviewer/version. Preview requires authentication or expiring signed access.
Publication triggers a bounded rebuild; show published-content version versus live-build
version and keep the prior release on failure. Removal invalidates the public build/cache.
Role permissions distinguish editing from approving/publishing legal or company claims.

TRAINING ENQUIRIES
Use the same lead pipeline with training-specific fields and consent record.
Private workshop enquiry does not reserve seats or accept payment.
Later cohort registration needs programme/session/capacity records, expiring seat
holds, idempotent registration, confirmation and cancellation workflow. Add payments
only after approved prices and terms; provider confirmation must govern enrolment state.

BOOKING INTEGRATION LATER
Prefer an accessible link to the selected scheduling service before loading a heavy
embed. Confirm account ownership, working hours, timezone and privacy implications.
Signed provider events can update an appointment linked to the lead. Handle booking,
reschedule and cancellation separately and deduplicate retries. Clicking "Book" is
not proof of a confirmed meeting; record conversion only after actual confirmation.

OPTIONAL AI ASSISTANT LATER
Not required for the website launch. If justified, restrict answers to approved site
content, show source links, mark uncertainty and offer a human enquiry route.
No access to private leads/customer documents; no autonomous quoting, purchasing,
emailing or contract promises. Rate/budget limits, redacted logging and human handoff.
Describe actual deployment status; a service advertisement does not require a live chatbot.

07. DATA MODEL AND API CONTRACTS
===============================
Launch entities:
users: staff identity reference, email, status, MFA/session metadata as appropriate.
roles/user_roles: server-side administrative permissions.
leads: reference, name, email, company/phone optional, service, message, source,
       status, owner, optional qualification JSON, created_at, updated_at, version.
lead_consents: purpose, notice version, consent/acknowledgement value and timestamp.
lead_notes: lead_id, author_id, text, timestamps; internal only.
lead_status_events: previous/new status, actor, reason and timestamp.
submission_keys: key hash, payload hash, lead reference and expiry.
notification_jobs: lead_id, kind, payload reference, status, attempts, next_attempt_at.
notification_deliveries: provider ID, delivery state, timestamps and safe error code.
audit_events: actor, action, object, timestamp and appropriately redacted change summary.

Later entities:
company_profiles, service_pages, case_studies, testimonials_with_permissions,
training_programmes, training_sessions, training_registrations, articles, tags,
media_assets, content_versions, publication_jobs and appointment_events.
Do not store a testimonial without provenance/permission and approval status.

Constraints:
- Unique enquiry reference and scoped idempotency key.
- Checked service/status values; database foreign keys for leads/notes/owners.
- Index lead created_at, status, service, owner, and due follow-up dates.
- Store UTC timestamps; render training/booking times in an explicit timezone.
- Version-check concurrent edits; maintain append-only status/audit records.
- Separate staff authentication data from public content projections.
- Define retention/deletion workflows before launch; avoid indefinite raw request logs.

API prefix /api/v1; JSON envelope {data,meta}; errors
{error:{code,message,fields,requestId}}. Use bounded pagination and request IDs.

POST /enquiries
Public, validated, rate-limited and idempotent.
Input: name, email, service, message, optional company/phone/budget/timeframe,
training/AI qualification fields, privacyNoticeVersion, optional marketingConsent,
submissionToken and idempotency header. Never accept client-defined owner/status.
Output 201: {data:{reference,status:"received"}}; matching retry returns stored result.
422 for field errors; 429 with retry guidance; 409 for key/payload mismatch.
Do not expose whether a submitted email already appears in the lead database.

GET /health
Public minimal liveness only; no database credentials/configuration details.
Internal readiness endpoint checks required services for deployment/monitoring.

GET /admin/leads
Lead manager/admin, paginated filtered projection.
GET /admin/leads/:id
Authorised staff only; full lead plus notes/history.
PATCH /admin/leads/:id
Authorised staff; allowlisted fields, expected version, audit event.
POST /admin/leads/:id/notes
Authorised staff; bounded plain text; never silently exposed to visitor.
POST /admin/leads/export
Explicit permission; bounded export job, expiring download, audit entry.
GET /admin/notifications/failures
Authorised operator; safe delivery errors without full provider secrets/payloads.
POST /admin/notifications/:id/retry
Authorised operator; idempotent, no duplicate successful deliveries.

Later:
POST /webhooks/email       provider signature, duplicate-event protection
POST /webhooks/booking     provider signature, lead/appointment scope
POST /admin/content       content editor; creates draft only
POST /admin/content/:id/publish  publisher permission; approved version + build job
POST /training/enquiries   optional alias to the same validated lead intake service

Keep public status lookups private: no GET /enquiries/:reference exposing contact data.
A reference is for communication, not a bearer credential to the entire lead record.
Avoid dynamic content endpoints until a CMS is selected; static content can ship in HTML.

08. SECURITY, INTEGRATIONS AND OPERATIONS
=======================================
Separate web assets, API, PostgreSQL and email provider credentials. Serve behind TLS
with restricted CORS and secure cookie/CSRF settings for the chosen auth arrangement.
Parameterised queries, server validation, output escaping, restrictive security headers,
rate limits and appropriate session expiration/revocation.
VITE_* variables are public browser configuration; no mail key/database URL belongs there.
Use secret storage or deployment environment variables for server credentials.

Data minimisation:
Collect only what helps answer the enquiry. No ID card/passport upload, confidential
project archive, model API key or client database required to initiate a conversation.
Analytics must not include form message, name, email, phone or private lead identifiers.
Do not send typed form content to session replay tools. Disable replay on contact/admin.
Marketing opt-in remains separate and revocable. Final privacy/cookie/retention choices
must be reviewed against actual business practices and applicable Malaysian requirements;
this planning document does not provide a legal compliance certification.

Third-party integrations:
Transactional email is the only required external integration at launch.
DNS sender authentication and a verified sending identity must be configured for the
approved company domain. Provider account and destination inbox remain open decisions.
WhatsApp is optional: show it only after Ryan confirms the business number. Use an
ordinary outbound link with a non-sensitive editable message; no unsolicited sending.
Maps optional: an address link is enough if an approved visitable office exists.
CRM sync optional: durable outbox/retries, scoped credentials and explicit data mapping.
Scheduling optional: booking confirmation handled as described above.
Analytics optional: choose purpose and consent handling before adding tags.
Do not load every integration on every route.

Analytics event plan:
service_view(service_slug), service_finder_select(service_slug),
contact_start(source_route,service_slug), contact_validation_error(field_code),
enquiry_received(service_slug), training_enquiry_received, outbound_contact_click.
Use coarse categories and aggregated metrics; deduplicate enquiry conversions from
the server acceptance event, not every visit to /thank-you.
Internal lead stages support sales reporting with restricted access; avoid public
dashboards implying business results not yet recorded.

Deployment/maintenance plan:
- Separate development/staging/production; staging has noindex plus access restriction
  when it contains private drafts. robots.txt alone is not access control.
- CDN/static host for pre-rendered web output; managed API runtime and database.
- Build/check/test in CI, apply reviewed migrations, deploy API compatible with the
  current frontend, then release static assets; retain rollbackable prior versions.
- Keep dependency lockfiles, reviewed updates, environment templates and backups.
- Monitor enquiry error rate, worker backlog, failed notifications, server errors and
  public uptime; redact personal fields from monitoring payloads.
- Test restore and manual follow-up from the lead database if email delivery fails.
- Document who receives incidents and who updates services/contact/legal content.
- Never serve the development Vite server as the production website.
